## Why this version is different

Rewritten as **confirm-or-correct** rather than open questions. You already
know the answers from your training and experience. What you don't have is
those answers in a form an insurance underwriter or an IRS reviewer can read
without taking your word for it.

A state employee can reply to this in thirty seconds by typing "1 through 5
correct." That is a far higher chance of a reply than six open questions, and
the reply is exactly the document you need.

The one genuinely open item, minors, is last and clearly marked.

## Email body

Ms. Miller,

I hold a current Commercial Nuisance Animal Permit (#[NUMBER], expires [DATE]) and WCO certification. I provide free, no-kill snake relocation and public education in the Greater Richmond area under the name SaveSnakes.

I am formalizing this work as a Virginia nonstock nonprofit corporation and applying for 501(c)(3) recognition. Our insurance carrier and the IRS will both want written confirmation of what the permit authorizes, so rather than ask open questions I have set out my understanding below. If you could reply confirming or correcting each point, that would be enormously helpful, and a short reply is completely fine.

My understanding:

1. **Supervision.** An individual who does not hold a CNAP may assist with the capture of an animal, but may not transport it off the property where it was captured. Off-property transport must be performed by a CNAP holder.

2. **Individual permits only.** The permit is issued to individuals. An organization cannot hold one. My permit remains valid as issued when I am performing permitted activity on behalf of a nonprofit corporation, and no separate organizational permit or endorsement exists.

3. **No-fee operation.** A CNAP is required for this activity regardless of whether a fee is charged. Providing the service free of charge does not remove the permit requirement.

4. **Release site permission.** Written landowner permission is not required at the release site for snake relocation. I ask specifically about this one because at least one publicly circulating copy of the permit conditions states that written permission is required, and I would like to know which reflects the current conditions.

5. **Recordkeeping.** Required activity records are reported to DWR on an annual form.

One question I have not been able to answer:

6. **Minors.** Is there a minimum age to hold a CNAP? Separately, may a person under 18 be present at a job site in a non-handling role such as documentation, equipment handling, or public education, while under the supervision of a permit holder? We expect to involve high school students in our education program and want to set our internal policy to match yours.

If any of this is better answered by a wildlife conflict biologist, please feel free to forward it and let me know who picked it up.

Thank you for your time.

## When the reply arrives

File it in the corporate records binder. It does three jobs:

| Use | What it establishes |
|---|---|
| Insurance underwriting | Scope of permitted activity, and who may perform which part |
| IRS Form 1023 narrative | That operations are conducted under state permit |
| Field operations policy (Bylaws 10.5) | The legal basis for your volunteer and youth boundaries |

Point 1 is the one to read most carefully when it comes back. It determines
whether your volunteer program adds capacity or only adds quality.
