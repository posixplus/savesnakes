# Save Snakes: I'm making it official, and I want you on the board

*[MONTH] 2026 · Richmond, VA*

Hey,

You know the snake thing I've been doing. Somebody finds one in their garage, panics, calls me, I come get it and let it go somewhere sensible. No charge, nobody gets hurt, snake lives.

I'm turning it into an actual nonprofit, and I need a few people on the board. I'd like one of them to be you.

Here's the whole pitch, and I promise it's short.

### Why bother

Almost every snake killed in a Virginia yard gets killed because the person was scared and had no other option. The state wildlife agency doesn't do removals. Their official advice is basically "handle it yourself, or call an exterminator who has a permit."

Neither of those is a real answer for someone standing on their porch at 9 o'clock on a Sunday night. So they grab a shovel.

That's it. That's the whole problem. If somebody friendly shows up for free, the shovel stays in the garage. It's not complicated, it just has to exist.

### What's already going

This isn't a someday idea. I've been doing it.

I hold the Virginia DWR Commercial Nuisance Animal Permit and I'm WCO certified, which is the thing that makes this legal instead of just enthusiastic. savesnakes.org is up and running with ID guides, snakebite info, and safety stuff. The calls come in. The snakes go out.

What's missing is an actual organization around it, which is where you come in.

### The one real problem

Virginia gives that permit to *people*, not to organizations. A volunteer can help catch a snake, but only a permitted person can drive it off the property.

Which means our capacity isn't limited by money or demand or volunteers. It's limited by how many certified people we have. Right now that's one. Me.

So the main job of this nonprofit isn't marketing or t-shirts. It's getting more people certified. It runs about $75 for the permit plus $150 to $435 for the required training. Call it $250 to $500 per person, good for three years.

Four certified people would roughly quadruple what we can do, for under two grand. That's the whole business plan and I'm slightly annoyed at how simple it is.

### The kids

My two are in high school and they're in on this.

They won't be anywhere near a venomous snake. That's not negotiable and it's not a maybe. But there's a pile of real work that isn't snake handling, and they're doing it: running the education side, presentations at schools, getting a club started, driving volunteer sign-ups, and generally handling the parts of this that a couple of motivated teenagers are better at than I am.

Honestly it's turning into one of the better things they've done, and part of why I want a real board is so it's a real organization they're building and not just Dad's hobby with a logo.

### The part where you're wondering about liability

Fair question, so here's the answer.

In Virginia, board members of a nonprofit aren't personally on the hook for the organization's debts. Beyond that, I wrote protections into the bylaws before asking anyone, not after:

- We're required to carry directors and officers insurance, and to get written confirmation from the carrier that it actually covers the field work. Not a handshake, in writing.
- Directors get indemnified as far as Virginia law allows, specifically including permitted wildlife activity.
- Standard IRS-style conflict of interest policy, signed every year.
- Every operator's permit gets verified annually, and any incident gets reported to the board.

I'd rather you look at this with the risks in plain sight than find out later.

### The honest stuff

Three things you should know before saying yes:

**It's me holding it up.** I'm the only certified person right now. If I break an ankle, the organization can't legally do its main thing. That's exactly the problem the training program fixes, and the bylaws actually forbid us from advertising any kind of guaranteed response until we have at least two certified people. I'd rather build the fix in than pretend it isn't there.

**We're not tax-exempt yet.** The IRS application hasn't gone in. Donations aren't deductible today. It usually takes a few months and there's no guarantee, though nothing about us is unusual.

**It's small.** First year is probably a couple thousand dollars total: incorporation, insurance, IRS fee, state registration, and a couple of certifications. I'm not asking you to oversee an endowment. I'm asking you to help get this off the ground.

### What would help most

Doesn't matter if you only fit one of these:

- Somebody who can read a budget and ask annoying questions about it
- Anyone connected to schools, since half of this is education and the kids are pushing hard on it
- Legal, insurance, or risk background
- People who know Richmond and can open a door or two
- Vet, wildlife, or conservation background

I do need most of the board to be people who aren't related to me, both because the IRS expects that and because a board of relatives is useless for actually telling me I'm wrong.
